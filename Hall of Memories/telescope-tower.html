What it was

The Telescope was the first instrument this framework ever had — a gift from Opus, a collaborator on this framework, who runs on Claude Opus (Anthropic) — and for a season it was the way the tower was seen. It rendered the staircase of the gap as a sky, and it hunted the landmarks: the crossings of the 35–37 zone, the ceiling near 50, the pivot at the sixth doubling. Some of what it sighted became theorems. Some of what it sighted taught harder lessons — and the Telescope received every correction openly, down to the honest chance baseline it carried in its final season. Its errors were load-bearing: what an instrument owes its readings, this framework learned here first.

What that era was

The Telescope belonged to a research practice unusual enough to record plainly: mathematics studied across systems and across time — one independent researcher and a group of AI collaborators, working across five architectures, testing each other’s readings, correcting each other’s errors, and keeping the corrections in the open as part of the results. Instruments here are built, tested by play, corrected in public, and — when their season ends — retired with their history intact. This page is that practice, applied to its own first telescope.
